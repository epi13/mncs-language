# Terminology

## Component

A named semantic unit with an interface, implementation, obligations, authority, and evidence relationships.

## Contract

A behavioral property associated with a component. Initial kinds include `requires`, `ensures`, `invariant`, `preserves`, and `budget`.

## Effect

An observable interaction with state or an external system, such as reading a file, modifying a balance, using randomness, or connecting to a service.

## Capability

Explicit authority that permits a class of effects. Possessing a capability does not prove correct use; it establishes that the operation is authorized within the declared boundary.

## Evidence-bearing capability

A typed value or semantic relationship demonstrating that a bounded property has been established for an exact subject and scope, such as alignment, bounds, region separation, liveness, authorization, or path confinement. It remains dependent on its construction rule, verifier, assumptions, and material identities.

## Assumption

A named proposition consumed by a claim but not established inside the current proof boundary.

## Evidence claim

A relationship stating that a verifier has claimed, tested, analyzed, verified, or externally verified a property.

## Evidence artifact

A durable output supporting an evidence claim, ideally content-addressed and bound to the relevant subject and verifier version.

## Micro-verifier

A small verifier designed to answer one bounded question against a narrow semantic or IR region.

## Machine-intent expression

A semantic operation that combines exact behavior with machine-oriented intent, required facts and capabilities, permitted lowering choices, target limits, and evidence obligations.

## Machine intent

The reason a machine property or realization is desired, such as vectorization, cache locality, branch elimination, constant-time behavior, bounded allocation, or target specialization. Intent does not prove that the property was achieved.

## Fact

A proposition established for an exact subject and scope by language rules, proof, analysis, a runtime check, or evidence-bearing capability. Facts consumed by lowering become stale when their material dependencies change.

## Preference

A non-semantic input to candidate ordering or lowering selection. A preference may be ignored without changing required behavior and cannot satisfy an obligation.

## Requirement

A semantic property that every accepted realization must preserve.

## Obligation

A stable proposition generated by a semantic operation or transformation rule and resolved as `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `UNKNOWN` under a declared method and scope.

## Lowering envelope

The bounded set of transformation classes and realizations permitted for a semantic operation while preserving its requirements.

## Portability envelope

The target architectures, features, data layouts, ABIs, compiler/backend semantics, runtime conditions, and fallback behavior for which a realization and its evidence apply.

## Reference semantics

A clear executable, relational, mathematical, or contractual meaning against which one or more realizations may be checked. A reference retains its own assumptions and trust boundary.

## Realization

A concrete implementation or lowering candidate for a stable semantic operation, often bound to a target and portability envelope.

## Backend promise

An optimization-relevant claim communicated to a conventional backend, such as no-overflow, in-bounds, non-aliasing, alignment, floating-point relaxation, or reduced atomic ordering. It may be emitted only from established semantics or current evidence.

## Trust boundary

The point at which an argument depends upon an assumption, external component, unsafe region, foreign implementation, verifier, compiler, runtime, or hardware behavior not established by the current evidence.

## Invalidation

The process of determining which claims and artifacts are no longer reliable after a change to source, semantics, dependencies, assumptions, verifier versions, environments, target profiles, lowering rules, compiler pipelines, or artifacts.

## Assurance level

A declared evidence state. Initial vocabulary includes claimed, tested, analyzed, verified, and externally verified.

## Semantic identity

An identity derived from canonical program meaning rather than source spelling alone.

## Verified SSA

A static-single-assignment representation whose transformations, machine-intent operations, and optimization-relevant claims are linked to explicit obligations or evidence.
