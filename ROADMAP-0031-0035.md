# Roadmap Supplement — RFC 0031 through RFC 0035

This supplement closes the roadmap gap between the lifecycle work in RFC 0030 and the next foundational compiler/language-evolution work by explicitly connecting RFCs 0031–0035.

It is intentionally additive to `ROADMAP.md`. The older roadmap remains the historical milestone document; this supplement records the newer cross-cutting semantic tracks until they are consolidated into a future roadmap rewrite.

The sequence is:

```text
RFC 0031
Derivation / provenance / supply-chain validity
        |
        v
RFC 0032
Quantitative facts / budgets / feasibility
        |
        v
RFC 0033
Preference / risk / realization selection
        |
        v
RFC 0034
Empirical evidence generation / experiment semantics
        |
        v
RFC 0035
Elaboration / binding / inference / coherence
```

These tracks are not a simple pipeline. They are mutually recursive evidence and realization layers. The ordering above states primary ownership and the current research dependency direction.

## Active cross-cutting track — build, derivation, reproducibility, and supply-chain evidence

[RFC 0031](rfcs/0031-machine-native-build-derivation-reproducibility-artifact-lineage-supply-chain-attestation-semantics.md) establishes explicit derivation and artifact lineage rather than treating a CI job, package name, signature, or release tag as sufficient origin semantics.

Current design work:

- distinguish artifact identity, derivation identity, build-execution identity, provenance identity, and release identity;
- model derivations as explicit graphs over source, tool, configuration, environment, generated, packaging, and verification inputs;
- distinguish declared inputs from observed influence and dependency closure from toolchain/bootstrap/influence closure;
- model hermeticity by dimension rather than one boolean;
- distinguish deterministic/repeatable/independently reproducible builds;
- expose source provenance separately from build provenance;
- model attestations, signatures, transparency records, SBOMs, and publication authorization as related but non-equivalent artifacts;
- make bootstrap seeds/trust roots explicit and allow DDC-style source/binary correspondence evidence;
- bind cache reuse to derivation/artifact correspondence rather than cache-key presence;
- keep untrusted build-plan generation separate from narrow derivation checking.

1.0 threshold highlights:

- same derivation across distinct executions with identical bytes;
- distinct derivations producing identical bytes without fabricated shared origin;
- undeclared host influence refuting hermeticity;
- local repeatability succeeding while independent reproducibility fails;
- signature-valid/provenance-incomplete promotion rejection;
- source-governance pass with unauthorized-builder failure;
- explicit opaque bootstrap root;
- transparency inclusion without automatic authorization;
- cache artifact bound to the wrong derivation rejected;
- untrusted build plan independently checked.

## Active cross-cutting track — quantitative cost, resource accounting, QoS, energy, and performance

[RFC 0032](rfcs/0032-machine-native-complexity-quantitative-cost-resource-accounting-qos-energy-performance-semantics.md) establishes typed quantitative relations over explicit resources rather than one universal `cost` scalar.

Current design work:

- distinguish dimension, unit, metric, aggregation, workload, target, and observation model;
- give each metric an explicit resource/composition algebra;
- distinguish work, span, wall time, throughput, latency, peak use, cumulative use, rate, and capacity;
- support exact, interval, upper/lower, symbolic, asymptotic, amortized, expected, percentile, and empirical quantities without conflation;
- model capacity, reservation, allocation, consumption, utilization, attribution, and budgets separately;
- support work/span, AARA/potential, sized-resource, graded/quantitative typing, queueing, network-calculus, WCET, Roofline, probabilistic reward, and QoS theories selectively;
- keep benchmark observations distinct from analytical bounds;
- model energy, power, carbon, and monetary valuation separately;
- quantify verification/proof/build/migration/model-call costs;
- determine hard quantitative feasibility before RFC 0033 selection.

1.0 threshold highlights:

- work is not time;
- cumulative allocation is not peak live memory;
- amortized is not worst-case;
- analysis `UNKNOWN` is not unbounded;
- benchmark maximum is not WCET;
- percentile is not universal bound;
- service cost is not queue response time;
- power is not energy and energy is not carbon;
- proof generation is not proof checking;
- a semantically valid realization can be quantitatively inadmissible;
- multiple candidates must survive so preference remains RFC 0033's problem.

## Active cross-cutting track — multi-objective realization search, risk, preference, and selection

[RFC 0033](rfcs/0033-machine-native-realization-search-multi-objective-optimization-pareto-risk-preference-selection-policy-semantics.md) establishes that feasibility and measurement do not themselves create preference.

Current design work:

- apply semantic/assurance/hard quantitative feasibility before preference;
- distinguish hard constraints, soft constraints, objectives, normalization, and policy;
- represent Pareto dominance, candidate sets/fronts, and exact versus approximate Pareto knowledge;
- support weighted, lexicographic, epsilon-constraint, reference-point, aspiration/reservation, and satisficing policies explicitly;
- preserve partial preferences, ties, incomparability, and unresolved decisions;
- bind preference provenance and constraint-relaxation authority to RFC 0025;
- distinguish probability, uncertainty, and risk appetite;
- support expected, tail-risk/CVaR, robust, distributionally robust, and minimax-regret policies;
- model safe exploration, surrogate search, bandits, value of information, stopping rules, optimality gaps, and bounded search;
- support adaptive/portfolio/randomized selection while preserving switching/lifecycle obligations;
- keep untrusted optimization/search separate from narrow policy/selection checking.

1.0 threshold highlights:

- Pareto-optimal does not imply selected;
- hard constraints cannot be hidden as large penalties;
- weighted and lexicographic policies may legitimately choose different winners from identical facts;
- `TIED`, `INCOMPARABLE`, and `UNRESOLVED` are distinct;
- heuristic/bounded search cannot claim global optimum;
- mean-optimal and CVaR-optimal candidates may differ;
- robust and nominal policies may differ;
- value-of-information may choose another experiment instead of a candidate;
- unauthorized constraint relaxation is rejected;
- untrusted selection is independently checked.

## Active cross-cutting track — test, fuzz, coverage, experiment, benchmark, oracle, and empirical evidence

[RFC 0034](rfcs/0034-machine-native-test-fuzz-coverage-experiment-benchmark-oracle-empirical-evidence-semantics.md) establishes testing and experimentation as scoped evidence-producing computation rather than scalar pass/fail metadata.

Current design work:

- distinguish test cases, executions, observations, oracle evaluations, results, and assurance appraisal;
- preserve the asymmetry between finite successful samples and concrete universal counterexamples;
- make generator identity/distribution/seed/size/boundary policy part of evidence identity;
- model oracle applicability/dependencies independently of subject correctness;
- support property, differential, metamorphic, mutation, combinatorial, model-based, symbolic/concolic, fuzz, schedule, and fault experiments;
- distinguish structural, MC/DC, t-way, semantic, schedule, fault, mutation, and requirement coverage;
- keep coverage separate from correctness and adequacy;
- model shrinking/delta debugging with scoped minimality;
- make flakiness/replay/rediscovery/failure clustering/regression lineage explicit;
- use rigorous statistical design, repetition hierarchy, sequential testing, and benchmark methodology;
- tie experiment harness/oracle provenance to RFC 0031;
- tie measured metric meaning to RFC 0032 and information-action decisions to RFC 0033;
- keep untrusted experiment generation separate from narrow experiment/result checking.

1.0 threshold highlights:

- finite passing samples remain bounded support;
- one valid counterexample can refute a universal claim;
- faulty oracle disagreement does not automatically identify a guilty subject;
- 100% structural coverage can coexist with a bug;
- branch coverage is not MC/DC;
- t-way coverage remains model-relative;
- bounded fuzz/symbolic/schedule/fault campaigns remain bounded evidence;
- retries do not erase failed/flaky attempts;
- statistically significant need not mean practically important;
- insufficient power remains inconclusive;
- stale empirical evidence invalidates only dependent claims.

## Active cross-cutting track — elaboration, scope, binding, inference, constraints, defaults, and coherence

[RFC 0035](rfcs/0035-machine-native-elaboration-scope-binding-inference-constraint-default-coherence-resolution-semantics.md) establishes elaboration as an evidenced relation from conveniently incomplete source to explicit semantic meaning.

Current design work:

- distinguish parsing, name resolution, elaboration, constraint generation, solving, defaulting, and lowering;
- use semantic binding IDs rather than textual names as identity;
- model scope graphs/resolution paths/witnesses and phase-sensitive visibility;
- make the elaboration environment and elaboration/default/coherence profile explicit and versioned;
- use bidirectional typing and local inference as the initial inference discipline;
- represent metavariables and typed semantic holes explicitly;
- distinguish missing semantic content from intentionally deferred realization variables;
- represent generated constraints and their source provenance in a dependency graph;
- preserve `SAT`, `UNSAT`, and `UNKNOWN` solver outcomes;
- use specialized solver families with explicit cross-solver facts rather than one opaque inference engine;
- preserve principal solutions where available without assuming universal principality;
- elaborate interface/typeclass-like obligations into explicit evidence terms;
- enforce coherence/specificity rather than search-order resolution;
- model open-world versus closed-world evidence search;
- insert coercions/defaults explicitly and record their provenance;
- distinguish deterministic elaboration from semantic coherence;
- treat ambiguity as a valid compiler result;
- preserve intentional representation/location/realization freedom for later RFCs;
- keep AI/solver/heuristic elaboration outside the core trust boundary when explicit core checking is possible.

1.0 threshold highlights:

- versioned scope/binding/reference/resolution witnesses;
- alpha-equivalent renaming preserving meaning;
- ambiguous imports cannot be resolved by incidental order;
- bidirectional type checking and expected-type-guided literal inference;
- first-order unification and explicit metavariables;
- a principal type case and a no-principal-solution case;
- explicit constraint graph with source origins and `SAT`/`UNSAT`/`UNKNOWN`;
- missing capability cannot be repaired by fabricated authority;
- interface evidence is inserted explicitly;
- overload resolution uses applicability/specificity rather than enumeration order;
- ambiguous/coherent coercion paths are distinguished;
- default profile/version contributes to elaboration identity;
- typed semantic holes are distinct from realization-deferred variables;
- search-order permutation cannot change meaning under a coherence claim;
- independent elaborators or strategies are checked under an RFC 0020 relation;
- Forge can repair an explicit ambiguity/hole/conflict and a smaller checker validates the resulting core.

## Consolidated 1.0 integration principle

The language should not reach the research 1.0 threshold merely because individual RFC prototypes exist.

At least one end-to-end study should demonstrate:

```text
source
  -> RFC 0035 explicit elaboration witness
  -> existing semantic/core checks
  -> RFC 0031 reproducible/provenanced artifact derivation
  -> RFC 0034 scoped empirical campaigns
  -> RFC 0032 explicit quantitative facts and hard feasibility
  -> RFC 0033 authorized multi-objective selection
  -> RFC 0030 lifecycle transition/deployment
```

with no stage laundering the claim class of another stage.

In particular:

- inference cannot replace proof;
- testing cannot replace proof;
- measurement cannot create preference;
- preference cannot repair invalid semantics;
- a signature cannot replace provenance;
- provenance cannot establish correctness;
- a faster candidate cannot bypass a hard requirement;
- an optimizer cannot invent authority;
- an elaborator cannot invent authority;
- unresolved search cannot be reported as impossibility;
- bounded evidence cannot be reported as universal evidence.

This supplement should eventually be folded into a consolidated `ROADMAP.md` rewrite once RFC 0036 language/specification evolution semantics establish the versioning/migration rules needed to evolve the roadmap and specification together.
